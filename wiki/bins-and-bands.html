autotune · how it works

Bins and bands

AutoTune EQ runs on Android's own equaliser. It splits the sound into slots that are all the same width in Hz. AutoTune's bands grow with frequency: narrow in the bass, wide in the treble. A band only does its job when it is at least one slot wide.

What each setting changes

  • Bigger blocks give narrower slots, so the bass bands work. The cost is delay: the phone holds back one block of sound before it plays (4096 samples is about 85 ms). Changes to the EQ also take effect in steps of half a block.
  • Smaller blocks play sooner, but the slots get wide. At 512 samples a slot is 94 Hz, so everything below about 140 Hz is just two slots. The bass is smeared and the 45 Hz band sets everything from 47 to 140 Hz.
  • More bands (1/6 octave instead of 1/3; the app calls it the 61-band setting) can follow narrower cuts, but each band is narrower too, so they need finer slots before they work in the bass.
  • Sample rate moves the slots a little. A slot is the sample rate divided by the block size, so at 44.1 kHz slots are about 8% narrower than at 48 kHz. Android rounds the block up to a power of two, so AutoTune's request of 80 ms gives 4096 samples at both rates.
  • The truck itself sets a floor no setting can fix: below about 30 Hz its speakers barely play, and what you hear there is mostly overtones and rattle.

Words used here

Slot (bin)
One of the equal-width pieces Android splits the sound into. Each slot gets one gain.
Band
One of AutoTune's EQ controls. Android hands each slot to the first band whose edge reaches it.
Octave
A doubling of frequency, such as 50 to 100 Hz or 1 to 2 kHz. A 1/6-octave band is about 12% of its frequency wide: 5 Hz at 40 Hz, 116 Hz at 1 kHz.
Block
How many samples Android processes at once. Slot width = sample rate ÷ block.
Sample rate
Samples per second of the phone's output, usually 48 000.
Delay
How much later the sound comes out with AutoTune EQ on. About one block.
